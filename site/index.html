Problem

Inbound checks are fragmented and exception visibility is poor. Barcode, SKU, quantity, duplicate-pallet, capacity, and device checks happen at different points, so nobody can say with confidence whether a pallet is safe to put away, who owns a failed movement, or whether inventory was updated before the pallet actually arrived.

Goal

A traceable pallet receipt and putaway flow with safe recovery: invalid pallets are stopped early, every WMS-to-WCS movement is traceable, exceptions are visible and bounded, and inventory changes exactly once, only after verified completion.

My role

Sole business analyst on a self-directed case study: problem framing, stakeholder and discovery planning, process modelling, requirements, data and interface contracts, acceptance criteria, UAT, and a clickable prototype used to validate the rules.

What I did

  • Framed the problem as 4 business needs and a fixed inbound-only scope; mapped 5 stakeholder personas with a proposed RACI and a 45-minute interview guide.
  • Modelled the assumed As-Is and proposed To-Be in BPMN 2.0, plus a system context, WMS–WCS sequence, and task state machine.
  • Wrote an SRS/FSD-lite: 10 functional requirements, 10 business rules, 5 NFRs, and a 13-code error catalogue.
  • Ordered a 12-story backlog by dependency and operational risk; each story passes an INVEST check and has positive and negative Given/When/Then criteria (26 in total).
  • Specified the data and interface contracts: 9-table SQLite schema with data dictionary, OpenAPI 3.1 (8 operations), and 10 SQL control and metric queries.
  • Validated the rules with a clickable prototype and 14 automated UAT cases, linked end to end in a requirements traceability matrix (BN → FR → BR → US → UAT).

1.Context and problem

The problem statement above is a design premise, not a measured baseline. The consequences below are hypotheses to test in real discovery:

  • Invalid pallets entering an automated putaway flow.
  • Incomplete movement history between the WMS and the warehouse control system (WCS).
  • Late recovery from capacity or device exceptions, with unclear ownership.
  • Inventory updated before physical completion, or updated twice.

3.Process analysis: As-Is to To-Be

The assumed As-Is relies on manual checks, spreadsheet lookups, and informal device coordination, so exceptions surface late and inventory is updated by hand.

The To-Be moves every check into the WMS, makes each movement traceable, and gives every failure a visible, bounded recovery path.

Sample user story (US-01 — Validation)

As an inbound operator, I want immediate validation of barcode, SKU, and quantity so that invalid receipt data cannot enter putaway.

AC-US-01-01 (positive). Given a new pallet ID, a valid barcode, a known active SKU, one lot, and quantity 10 — when the operator submits — then the WMS creates one pallet record and no movement task yet.

AC-US-01-02 (negative). Given a malformed barcode, an unknown or inactive SKU, or a quantity that is blank, fractional, zero, or negative — when submitted — then the WMS returns INVALID_BARCODE, UNKNOWN_OR_INACTIVE_SKU, or INVALID_QUANTITY and creates neither a pallet nor a task.

The backlog orders all 12 stories by dependency, then operational risk, then user value: validation and identity first, event idempotency before inventory commit, and evidence before metrics. No numeric scoring is used, because no effort or value inputs exist and invented numbers would be false precision.

5.Key decisions and assumptions

IDDecisionRuleWhy
DEC-01Deterministic putawayFirst compatible location below capacity, ordered by zone priority then location code.Explainable and testable; no claim of route optimisation.
DEC-02Completion-gated inventoryInventory moves only on a valid, unique TASK_COMPLETED event.Separates planned movement from physical completion; one audit point.
DEC-03Bounded retryMaximum two retries; the third failure escalates to a person.Prevents silent infinite retry and makes ownership explicit.
DEC-04UTC ISO 8601 timestampsAll simulator timestamps use UTC ISO 8601.Unambiguous ordering across WMS, WCS, and audit.

Three assumptions are locked but unvalidated, each with a validation owner and a revisit trigger: one SKU and lot per pallet (AS-01), positive integer quantity (AS-02), and capacity counted in whole pallets (AS-03). Retry limits, cancellation rights, and RACI remain proposals until a real decision owner confirms them.

6.Exception handling and controls

Every failure has a code, a visible outcome, and an owner. A task moves through CREATED → VALIDATED → DISPATCHED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED, with FAILED, TIMED_OUT, and CANCELLED reachable only through documented transitions.

Error codeObservable outcome
INVALID_BARCODE / UNKNOWN_OR_INACTIVE_SKU / INVALID_QUANTITYReceipt rejected; nothing is created.
DUPLICATE_PALLETExisting record unchanged; new receipt rejected.
NO_AVAILABLE_LOCATIONNo dispatch; capacity exception visible to the supervisor.
DEVICE_OFFLINE / PALLET_JAM / TASK_TIMEOUTTask fails or times out; active exception with a retry, cancel, or escalate path.
RETRY_LIMIT_REACHEDThird failure escalates; no third retry command is sent.
DUPLICATE_EVENTAcknowledged as duplicate; no second state or inventory change.
INVALID_TRANSITION / UNKNOWN_TASKRequest rejected; state and inventory unchanged; audit retained.
CANCELLATION_NOT_ALLOWEDCancellation from a terminal or undocumented state is rejected.

Data and interface contracts

  • Data model: 9-table SQLite schema with a data dictionary, synthetic seed data, and lineage notes.
  • Interface: OpenAPI 3.1 contract with 8 operations for receipt, task, event, retry, exception, inventory, audit, and metrics.
  • Controls: 6 SQL integrity checks (for example, duplicate pallets, inventory count per completed task, duplicate event IDs) and 4 metric queries.

8.Limitations and next steps

What this case study does not prove

  • No warehouse user or domain expert has validated the workflow; real discovery could change rules and priorities.
  • The WCS is a deterministic in-memory simulator, not a physical device or a third-party integration.
  • Persistence, authentication, concurrency, and production performance are intentionally out of scope.

What I would do first in a real engagement

  • Observe inbound work on site and run the interview guide with the five personas.
  • Collect real exception examples and validate the As-Is / To-Be flow against them.
  • Confirm data and integration contracts with the integration owner.
  • Update requirements, decisions, and UAT with named decision owners and sign-off.
